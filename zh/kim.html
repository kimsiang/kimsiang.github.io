Biography个人简介

I lead the Muon Physics Group at the Tsung-Dao Lee Institute (TDLI), Shanghai Jiao Tong University, where I am a Tenured Fellow and a Tenured Associate Professor in the School of Physics and Astronomy.

My work uses the muon to test the Standard Model of particle physics. I measure its magnetic anomaly in the Muon g-2 Experiment at Fermilab and search for its electric dipole moment with the muEDM experiment at PSI. Closer to home, my group is designing a high-repetition-rate muon source at Shanghai’s SHINE facility and using cosmic-ray muons to image the city’s underground spaces. I like to think of this as working across the full chain: from making muons, to measuring them, to putting them to use.

I grew up in Penang, Malaysia, and spent sixteen years studying and working abroad before coming to Shanghai: in Japan (Osaka University of Foreign Studies, Kyoto University, University of Tokyo), Switzerland (ETH Zurich, PhD 2015) and the USA (University of Washington, postdoc 2015–2019). Along the way I learned to do physics in five languages — Malay, Japanese, German, English and Chinese. I joined TDLI in 2019 and received tenure in 2025.

我是上海交通大学李政道研究所长聘学者、物理与天文学院长聘副教授，领导李政道研究所缪子物理课题组。

我的研究以缪子为探针检验粒子物理标准模型：在美国费米实验室缪子g-2实验中精确测量其反常磁矩，在瑞士保罗谢勒研究所（PSI）的muEDM实验中寻找其电偶极矩。在上海，课题组正在基于SHINE装置设计高重频缪子源，并利用宇宙线缪子对城市地下空间进行成像。从“产生缪子”到“测量缪子”再到“应用缪子”，我们希望打通这一完整链条。

我来自马来西亚槟城，来上海之前在海外学习和工作了16年：日本（大阪外国语大学、京都大学、东京大学）、瑞士（苏黎世联邦理工学院，2015年获博士学位）和美国（华盛顿大学，2015–2019年博士后）。这段经历让我能够用马来语、日语、德语、英语和汉语五种语言学习和开展物理研究。2019年加入李政道研究所，2025年获得长聘。

Working with me加入团队

We are a small, international team, and I try to keep the atmosphere open and curious. Each week we run a study group on introductory particle-physics textbooks, a journal club, and dedicated meetings for the Muon g-2 and muEDM collaborations. Students present their work at a national or international conference about twice a year, and strong graduate students can spend extended research periods at Fermilab and PSI.

Each year I take several undergraduates (PRP, Zhiyuan Scholars, Physics Research Practice and other programmes), two to five graduate students including international students, and postdocs. If you enjoy precision experiments, building detectors or turning data into physics, please write to me.
