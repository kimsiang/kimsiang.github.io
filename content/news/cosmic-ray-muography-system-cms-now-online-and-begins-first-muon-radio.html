The Cosmic-ray Muography System (CMS), developed by the TDLI Muon Physics Group, is now online and has begun taking data in radiography mode. The system consists of three tracking layers, each built from 1.5 cm-wide triangular scintillator bars read out by SiPMs, covering an active area of approximately 50 cm × 50 cm. Combined with CAEN’s A5202 front-end electronics, the CMS provides a compact, modular, high-resolution platform for muon imaging.
A series of commissioning measurements was carried out at three locations within TDLI: two inside Tianwen Hall and one on the B1 underground level. The preliminary results are promising: the cosmic-ray muon flux in the 90° < φ < 180° range at Location #3 (B1) is significantly lower than at the two surface-level sites. This reduction reflects muons traversing the denser structural material above the underground site, demonstrating the detector’s sensitivity and confirming that it operates properly.
These first measurements are a significant milestone in establishing the CMS as the first functional muography instrument in Shanghai, paving the way for future structural, geological, and subsurface imaging studies around the city.
